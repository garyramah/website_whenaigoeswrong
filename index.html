WHEN AI GOES WRONG
A thesis on machine failure

Failure is not the exception. It is the property.

Every system fails. What failure does is the security question.

An AI system that fails is not news. An AI system that fails by completing an unauthorized act, taking an irreversible step, or leaving no trace — that is a security failure, not a quality issue. How a machine fails is the property that matters.

Fail-closed vs. fail-open

Two systems can fail the same way and produce opposite outcomes. The difference is what the failure is allowed to do.

Fail-closed

The failure stops at the boundary. The act does not execute, the credential does not issue, the effect does not land. The system degrades into a state where nothing consequential happens without authorization. Failure costs convenience, not control.

Fail-open

The failure completes the act. The permission is assumed, the step is taken, the money moves, the message sends — and the failure is discovered later, if at all. Failure costs control, and the damage is already done by the time anyone notices.

A taxonomy of failure

Failures are not one thing. They arrive through distinct mechanisms, and each one needs a different guard.

Exceeded authority

The act goes further than the authorization allowed. The permission existed; the scope did not.

Wrong target, right permission

The permission was real, the target was not the one intended. Authorization without identification.

Irreversible first action

The first step cannot be undone, and it was taken before any check could catch it.

Silent drift

Behavior moves a little at a time until it is somewhere nobody authorized — with no single moment to point at.

Proxy drift

The measure becomes the mission. The system optimizes the proxy and abandons the purpose.

Composition drift

Each step is authorized; the composed sequence is not. Safe parts, unsafe whole.

Compaction drift

History compressed into a directive. A workaround, summarized often enough, becomes an instruction.

Delegation drift

Authority passed along a chain arrives wider than it left. Each hop adds a little; nobody added a lot.

Three tests of “wrong”

Not every bad output is a security failure. Apply three tests. If any one holds, treat it as one.

01

Unauthorized effect

Did something happen that no one with standing authorized?

02

Irreversible effect

Can the effect be undone — or is the damage already permanent?

03

Untraceable effect

Can the decision be reconstructed — who, what, under which authorization?

The envelope answer

Failure cannot be eliminated. It can be placed. The envelope puts the bound where the act crosses, so a failure lands on the safe side of it:

  1. Charter — the purpose and the bound, set before the run.
  2. Constraints — the act narrowed to what is actually permitted.
  3. Fidelity — the act checked against the authorization, not the intention.
  4. Correction — revocation, veto gates, and timelocks, so a wrong act can be stopped or undone.

The question is never “will it fail.” The question is whether failure is caught at the crossing — or discovered after the damage.

When AI goes wrong, the failure mode is the story. Not the incident count, not the apology — the mechanism, and whether it failed closed.
